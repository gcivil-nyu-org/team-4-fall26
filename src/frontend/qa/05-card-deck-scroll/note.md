Scrolling the homepage forced a synchronous layout for every card on every frame and restyled whole sections through inherited custom properties: the card deck wrote `--covered` on each holder and then read the next one's position, the phone section read its position after other code had written, every character of the intro re-resolved an unregistered var()/clamp() chain (about 16 ms a frame on its own), and the footer clouds and the cities' stars animated on the main thread even when off screen. Now positions are read in the scroll event and written in the next frame, the deck writes each card's own transform and filter, the intro's variables are registered `@property` numbers, and off-screen animations pause. In a 4 s wheel scroll in desktop Chrome forced layouts fell from 437 to 5 and script time from 6.7 s to 3.3 s; frames over 33 ms fell from about 188 to 128, and at 4x CPU the median frame went from 66.7 ms to 49.9 ms. The deck is pixel-identical (`before-*.png`, `after-*.png`); the intro differs by at most one colour level in 617 pixels, from numbers now computed as numbers. Not fixed: style and compositing for the intro's ~160 per-character 3D layers still cost most of each frame (see the proposals).
